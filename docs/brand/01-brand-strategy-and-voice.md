# Brand strategy and voice

## Brand architecture

**258 Web Co. sells the website service. The Website Factory is the system that helps deliver it.** This follows the [agency operating model](../agency-workflow.md) and gives each name one job.

| Name | Audience | What it stands for | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **258 Web Co.** | Small service businesses and referral partners | An independent studio that plans, designs, and builds clear business websites | Studio storefront, proposals, direct communication, sales collateral |
| **The Website Factory** | Ryan, technical collaborators, and prospective delivery partners evaluating the workflow | A local Hugo production system that takes a reviewed brief through reusable sections, a checked build, and a documented handoff | Repository, local workshop, technical walkthroughs, partner evaluation materials |
| **The client’s name** | The client’s customers | The client’s own business, services, and terms | Every delivered site and its customer-facing materials |

Use **The Website Factory** on first reference and **the Factory** in a sentence afterward. Use **258 Web Co.** with the period and a space after the number. Lead with one brand per piece. For a technical audience, the supporting sentence is “The Website Factory is the production system behind 258 Web Co.” Keep factory terms and marks out of client sites unless the project explicitly calls for an attribution.

## Primary audience and buying situation

| Audience | Trigger | What they need to decide | Proof to show | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary: owner or manager of a local service business** | An outdated website, a new business, a change in services, or a referral the current site fails to help | Can I explain what I do, see the scope, get through review, and know what happens after handoff? | A labeled sample in their line of work, a short scope, and an example handoff | Start a project |
| **Referral partner: designer, photographer, copywriter, or local IT provider** | A client needs a focused build and the partner wants a dependable handoff | Who handles content, the client relationship, and post-launch changes? | A sample scope, explicit responsibilities, and a short review checklist | Discuss a project |
| **Secondary: independent developer or small studio** | Repeated brochure-site setup, copy review, and delivery work | Can I follow the path from brief to working site and maintain the result? | A synthetic brief through a local build and an ejected Hugo folder | Review a sample build |

Start with businesses whose visitors need to understand services, confirm fit and coverage, and contact the business. A clinic, nonprofit, restaurant, or creator may fit when its scope is primarily informational. Projects requiring scheduling, payments, commerce, account access, sensitive intake, or frequent nontechnical editing need separate discovery and a deliberate tool choice. The local audience and offer sequence are positioning hypotheses. See [market and SWOT](02-market-and-swot.md) for the evidence and validation plan.

## Studio positioning

**Category**\
Independent web development studio for small service businesses.

**Positioning statement**\
For small service businesses that need a clearer website, 258 Web Co. plans and builds focused sites that explain the work, help visitors take the next step, and give the owner a clear handoff.

**Lead line**\
A clearer website for your service business.

**One-sentence pitch**\
We plan and build websites that show what you do, where you work, and how a customer can reach you.

**Short introduction**\
258 Web Co. is an independent web development studio for small service businesses. We start with what your customers need to know, agree on the scope, and build a site your team can review before launch. At handoff, we document the site files, hosting setup, and how to request or make changes.

| Message | Customer relevance | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Make the business easy to understand.** | Visitors can find the services, service area, and contact path. | Show the visitor’s route through a labeled demo. |
| **See the scope before the build.** | The owner can review pages, content responsibilities, and what comes later. | Show an example scope and the review questions. |
| **Know what you’re getting at handoff.** | The owner can plan updates, hosting, and future changes. | Show the handover record and project-specific delivery options. |

## Factory positioning

**Category**\
Local website production system for repeatable Hugo builds.

**Lead line**\
From reviewed brief to Hugo site.

**One-sentence pitch**\
The Website Factory turns a reviewed page plan into a checked Hugo site using reusable sections and an independent project copy.

**Technical introduction**\
Begin with a complete site starter or a blank page plan. Shape pages and sections around the brief, resolve the business facts, and compile the plan into a validated preset. Build a separate Hugo site and use the handoff tools to record what was checked and who owns the next step. Plain Hugo delivery is available through the existing eject workflow.

**Walkthrough story**\
Open a labeled fictional brief. Choose a starter, remove a section, and flag an unanswered question. Show the reviewed plan and the resulting site. End with the source folder, the check report, and the delivery instructions. Demonstrate any claimed release gate with a disposable fixture.

## Voice rules

- **Clear.** Start with the visitor or operator’s task. Explain what happens and name who does it. Use “service area,” “site files,” and “review the draft” in sales copy. Use “preset,” “compile,” and “eject” in the technical guide, with a short definition on first use.
- **Specific.** Explain what a check covers and what project it describes. A screenshot shows a layout. A dated report records the checks performed on a particular output. Use the precise version of the evidence in the claim.
- **Calm.** Write in short paragraphs. Offer a single next action. State questions plainly and preserve useful uncertainty. Use contractions where they sound natural.
- **Practical.** Name the deliverable and what the owner can do with it. Prefer “review the page list” to “start your digital journey.”
- **Warm.** “Tell us what you need your website to do” is a complete invitation. Use “we” for studio services and “you” for the reader’s choices.

| Situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Price is unresolved | “We’ll confirm the scope and price in writing before work starts.” |
| Fact is missing in a draft | “To be confirmed” plus the question and who can answer it. |
| Intake is unavailable | “Project enquiries are currently closed.” |
| Demo with fictional business facts | “Fictional example. Built to show the layout and content structure.” |
| A specific check completed | “We checked [page or release] for [specific behavior] on [date].” |
| Client approves the next step | “Review the draft pages and flag anything you’d like us to change.” |

Keep terms consistent across forms, headings, proposals, and replies. Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Use a verb plus an object for actions. Place error messages by the field they describe, tell the person how to correct them, and preserve entered values where the interface supports recovery.

## Claims and proof

Use the [claim and release procedure](07-brand-operations.md). Supported system mechanisms include versioned composition, local preview, separate client copies, and reviewed release controls. Treat speed savings, delivery turnaround, conversion gains, rankings, accessibility conformance, client endorsements, and current availability as claims that require their own evidence. Exact service prices, timelines, revision allowances, licensing, and support terms are owner decisions for a specific offer or engagement.

Use the lead lines as proposed copy. The factory identity and the studio campaign in this package are local working assets for Ryan’s review.
