# Sales and delivery templates

Use these as working copy. Fill bracketed fields from the brief or the owner’s confirmation. A short spoken answer is enough to start. Capture open facts once and carry them through the same project record.

## Offer structure

| Offer | Use when | Core scope to define | Separately agreed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Service business website** | A business needs a home for its services and contact details | Page list, key customer actions, copy ownership, design review, launch handoff | Photography, forms or CRM, migration, extra pages, care |
| **Landing page** | One service or campaign needs one clear action | Audience, offer, one page, destination, review, handoff | Ad management, testing, tracking, copy production |
| **Website redesign** | An existing site has unclear content or navigation | Content inventory, approved page plan, refreshed pages, redirect map, handoff | Deep migration, external integrations, large content archives |
| **Website care** | An owner wants a named path for routine updates | Change allowance, contact route, response terms, hosting responsibility | New features, emergency support, third-party fees |

Quote work after discovery. Record the build fee, hosting costs, recurring care, paid integrations, and change scope individually when they apply. Confirm that what the studio agrees to deliver can be maintained at the capacity available.

## Discovery note

> **Business and project**  
> [Business name, owner, contact path, current site]
>
> **Customer and task**  
> [Who is arriving and what do they need to do?]
>
> **Core pages and action**  
> [Name each page and the one main action.]
>
> **Facts to confirm**  
> [Service area, offer, prices, contact path, hours, credentials, image rights, or evidence for a claim.]
>
> **Responsibilities**  
> [Who supplies copy and photos? Who approves them? Who owns the domain, host, and updates?]
>
> **Scope and constraints**  
> [Any existing URLs, deadlines, accessibility needs, intake, integrations, or things to defer.]
>
> **Next decision**  
> [What needs an answer, who can give it, and when?]

Open with “What should a customer be able to do on the new site?” Ask about the present obstacle and end with the one unresolved issue that changes scope.

## Proposal

> # Website proposal for [business]
>
> **What the site should do**  
> [Describe the buyer’s goal and the main visitor task in plain language.]
>
> **Work included**  
> [List named pages, the amount of content and design work, approved inputs, responsive behavior, any contact route, review rounds, and agreed verification.]
>
> **Review and decision points**  
> [Dates or dependencies for brief confirmation, design review, content approval, prelaunch acceptance, and handoff. Identify who signs off.]
>
> **Fees and changes**  
> [Build amount, billing schedule, third-party fees, separately priced care, and how additional work will be approved.]
>
> **Delivery and updates**  
> [Name the artifact, hosting and domain owner, delivery and usage terms, access transfer, editing method, and follow-up scope.]
>
> **Work outside this scope**  
> [List relevant exclusions or later work, such as appointment scheduling or ongoing copy updates.]
>
> **Next step**  
> [How the client can accept this scope or request a change.]

Record accepted changes beside the original scope. Confirm agreed terms with the owner before making contractual or rights commitments.

## Handoff note

> **What you received**\
> [Site address or preview, delivery folder, named build tools, and dated check report.]
>
> **Who owns what**\
> [Domain, hosting, forms, site source, assets, accounts, and future updates. Share access through the agreed secure process.]
>
> **How to update the site**\
> [The files or interface to use, how to preview, and the person or route for help.]
>
> **How to check contact**\
> [Approved contact route, receipt owner, how receipt was verified, and date. Include status if intake remains pending.]
>
> **Open items**\
> [Outstanding content, checks, launch action, or accepted limitations.]
>
> **Next action**\
> [Single named action and owner.]

Use the factory’s handover report for technical evidence. For standalone Hugo delivery, use the [eject workflow](../operator-guide.md#final-delivery-without-the-factory), verify the delivered folder, and record it as the source of future edits.

## Partner evaluation

**What we can show**\
A synthetic brief, a checked build, and a standalone Hugo delivery folder.

**What we agree per project**\
Client communication, visible attribution, scope approval, project access, asset rights, and post-handoff changes.

**What a first engagement tests**\
Whether the partner can review, build, or maintain the agreed output with the delivered instructions and within their available capacity.
